# Phantom Portals

Phantom Portals is not a conventional dashboard. It is a modular technical command environment for the portable Phantom Portals IT platform — diagnostics, recovery, deployment, cybersecurity, networking, forensics, AI, automation, field utilities, WinPE and system orchestration.

Every interface should feel like another chamber inside the same machine.

**The target impression is a sophisticated classified technical command system — not a gaming interface.**

## The mantra

> Black builds the machine.
> Platinum delivers the information.
> Gold defines the Phantom.
> Crimson signals danger.
> Operator energy brings the system to life.
> Every effect must earn its place.

## The visual formula

| Share | Role |
| --- | --- |
| 55% | Black and graphite technical architecture — the structural foundation |
| 20% | Platinum and white instrumentation — typography, measurements, controls, information |
| 10% | Gold signature detailing — brand prestige and important structural lines |
| 5% | Crimson — danger, security tension, alert emphasis, atmosphere |
| 10% | Operator energy — contextual accents, portal effects, status, active-state feedback |

Directional rather than mathematical. The principle is what matters: **black dominates, platinum explains, gold identifies, crimson warns, operator colour activates.**

## Principles

**Build architecture, not decoration.** Interfaces should appear constructed — structural rails, panel seams, recessed regions, edge framing, engraved labels, segmented surfaces, controlled elevation, deliberate empty space. Not glowing cards on a dark background.

**Precision before spectacle.** This is operational software. Information stays readable, scannable, navigable, predictable and fast. Spectacle may amplify an important moment; it never obstructs one.

**Darkness is structure.** Black is not empty space. Five controlled dark materials communicate depth and hierarchy, rather than one flat dark background.

**Gold is precious.** Never a generic highlight. Phantom branding, major structural lines, premium controls, emblem details, section identifiers, important navigation architecture. Too much gold destroys its authority.

**Operator colours are energy.** They behave like current passing through the architecture — thin illuminated edges, active indicators, selected icons, graphs, portal energy, targeting marks, progress. They rarely become large fills, and they hold under 10–15% of the visible interface.

**Crimson means tension.** Danger, urgency, security. Never scattered.

**Typography is instrumentation.** Body text stays simple. Technical styling belongs in titles, numbers, identifiers, telemetry, labels and system codes.

**Motion has purpose.** Selection, activation, scanning, loading, targeting, navigation, transition, success, system response. Nothing animates because it can.

**Every screen has a focal point.** Where am I · which operator is active · what is the system doing · what needs attention · what action matters next.

## Operator energy

The seven canonical identities are permanent, and each is a theme in this system. Switch themes to see every `op*` token, rail, edge and glow resolve.

| Operator | Energy | Domain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phantom Core | `#39FF88` | Command, orchestration, system overview |
| IT Technician | `#00E5FF` | Hardware, diagnostics, deployment, Windows, repair |
| Cybersecurity | `#FF335F` | Security, defensive analysis, scanning, protection |
| Recovery | `#FF7700` | Rescue, backup, imaging, restoration |
| Forensics | `#A855F7` | Evidence, inspection, disk analysis, artifacts |
| Networking | `#14B8A6` | Connectivity, infrastructure, discovery |
| AI Operator | `#EC4899` | Models, knowledge systems, agents, automation |

**The operator does not redesign the interface.** The shell stays black, graphite, platinum and gold; the operator supplies energy. Each identity resolves as a five-step family so depth never requires an unrelated colour: `op` (base), `op-dim` (~50%), `op-bright`, `op-ghost` (14% — the only form in which operator colour covers area) and `op-glow`.

Destructive behaviour is never inferred from operator colour. A Cybersecurity screen is red; its delete button is still crimson.

## Brand geometry

Clipped corners, narrow chamfers, horizontal rails, vertical instrumentation lines, recessed slots, segmented frames, concentric rings, targeting arcs, portal circles, angled cuts, precision grid marks. Not rounded rectangles, bubbly UI, oversized pills or soft consumer geometry — the largest radius in the system is 3px.

**The Phantom Cut** — a diagonal chamfer on the upper-right or lower-left corner, at `cut-sm`, `cut-md` or `cut-lg`. Important panels, operator cards, command modules, modal architecture, large buttons. Never every element.

**The Phantom Line** — neutral line → gold node → operator-energy segment. Headers, module separators, panel titles, navigation, timelines. It is the single clearest signal that a surface belongs to this system.

## Typography

Three roles. **Command** for the system mark, operator titles and section identifiers — bold, geometric, controlled tracking, uppercase-capable, never novelty sci-fi. **Interface** for everything read and clicked; the dominant face. **Technical** monospace for telemetry, hashes, paths, logs, timestamps, addresses and every figure — never long prose.

Hierarchy: `system-title` → `operator-title` → `page-title` → `section-header` → `panel-header` → `body` → `telemetry` → `micro-label`.

Large numbers are instrumentation, not marketing metrics: a `readout` figure with a `micro-label` beneath it.

## Density

Three modes, changing spacing and row height but never legibility. **Comfortable** (`row-comfortable`) for landing, operator selection, settings and onboarding. **Standard** (`row-standard`), the default, for dashboards, tool browsers and the command center. **Dense** (`row-dense`) for logs, networking, diagnostics, forensic tables and inventory.

## Architecture

Five zones: **Command Masthead**, **Navigation Rail**, **Workspace**, **Context Panel**, **Telemetry Rail**. The workspace stays calm — task, tools, current data, context, telemetry, atmosphere, in that order of priority. Decorative elements rank last.

## The emblem

The Phantom emblem is the primary brand anchor, in four treatments.

**Hero** — the full rendered seal, for landing screens, splash, loading sequences and major branding surfaces. A raster asset under `assets/Logos/`, never redrawn in CSS.

**Interface** — the glyph and wordmark lockup, in the masthead and on operator entry.

**Glyph** — the ring bisected by a tapered spindle. The only treatment that survives below about 32px, and the only one that is vector, so it takes `sovereign-gold`, `highlight-gold` or operator energy from the tokens. It carries the masthead, the centre of the radial wheel, small controls and the favicon.

**Watermark** — the glyph at very low opacity behind shell geometry and empty states. Never a large glowing Hero emblem behind text.

The glyph is also the substituted letter in the wordmark, **PHANT&#216;M**. That substitution is the mark, not a typographic flourish, and it is never replaced with a plain O.

## The wordmark and the brand lines

The lockup reads **THE PHANT&#216;M** over **PHANTOM · PORTALS**, with the glyph leading.

The lockup is vector and scales from a single `font-size` — `PhantomWordmark` holds the horizontal, stacked and mono arrangements, the clear space rule and the minimum sizes. Clear space on every side is the glyph's width, and nothing enters it.

The wordmark is a logotype. It is *not* the Command typeface: the interface uses a bold geometric face for titles and section identifiers, while the rendered emblem's high-contrast display serif belongs only to the lockup. Setting UI titles in the wordmark face is the fastest way to make this system look like a poster. That serif is not bundled; the lockup is set in Command with the real glyph substituted in, and becomes final when the face is licensed and added under `fonts/`.

The brand lines, in the voice the emblem compositions establish:

> More than access. A higher perspective.
> Beyond borders. Higher control.
> Observe. Open. Operate. Transcend.

Used on landing, portal and branding surfaces. Never inside a workspace, and never as microcopy on a control.
